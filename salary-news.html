New NSSF Rates 2025: What Every Kenyan Employee Needs to Know

The National Social Security Fund (NSSF) Act 2013, upheld by the Court of Appeal, introduced a two-tier contribution system that significantly increased deductions compared to the old flat KES 200/month.

  • Tier I: 6% of the first KES 8,000 of gross salary = KES 480/month (employer matches)
  • Tier II: 6% of gross salary from KES 8,001 up to KES 72,000 = up to KES 3,840/month
  • Maximum employee contribution: KES 4,320/month for salaries of KES 72,000+
  • Employer matches the employee contribution — total of KES 8,640/month for high earners
  • For salaries below KES 8,000, NSSF is simply 6% of gross

This change means employees earning KES 72,000+ pay KES 4,120 more per month in NSSF than under the old rules. The funds are invested on your behalf for retirement.

SHIF 2025: How the Social Health Insurance Fund Affects Your Salary

From October 2024, the Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF) replaced the National Hospital Insurance Fund (NHIF). The change has a major impact on higher-income earners.

  • SHIF rate: 2.75% of gross salary — no cap or upper limit
  • Old NHIF: Was capped at KES 1,700/month regardless of salary
  • A KES 100,000 earner now pays KES 2,750/month instead of KES 1,700 — an increase of KES 1,050
  • A KES 200,000 earner pays KES 5,500/month in SHIF
  • Covers access to healthcare under the Social Health Authority (SHA)

SHIF is administered by the Social Health Authority and covers inpatient, outpatient, and emergency care. The higher contributions are intended to fund universal health coverage for all Kenyans.

Kenya PAYE Bands 2025/2026: Complete Explanation of All Tax Brackets

Pay As You Earn (PAYE) is deducted from your gross salary each month before it reaches your bank account. Understanding the bands helps you plan and negotiate better.

  • 10% on the first KES 24,000 of monthly taxable income
  • 25% on KES 24,001 – KES 32,333
  • 30% on KES 32,334 – KES 500,000
  • 32.5% on KES 500,001 – KES 800,000
  • 35% on income above KES 800,000
  • Personal Relief: KES 2,400/month (KES 28,800/year) reduces your PAYE bill

Taxable income is calculated after deducting NSSF, SHIF, and Housing Levy from gross salary. Most Kenyans earning under KES 30,000 gross effectively pay zero PAYE after personal relief.

Affordable Housing Levy Kenya: Who Pays, How Much, and What For?

The Affordable Housing Levy (AHL), introduced under the Finance Act 2023, is 1.5% of gross salary — paid by both employee and employer.

  • Employee contribution: 1.5% of gross salary (no cap)
  • Employer contribution: Additional 1.5% of your gross (employer-side cost)
  • A KES 50,000 earner pays KES 750/month; employer pays another KES 750
  • Funds go to the Affordable Housing Fund managed by the government
  • Beneficiaries: Kenyans in low- and middle-income brackets seeking affordable homes
  • The levy was upheld by the Supreme Court of Kenya in 2024

Unlike NSSF, the AHL is not a personal savings account — it funds a national housing programme. Employees cannot individually claim these funds back.

How to Read Your Kenyan Payslip: Every Line Item Explained

Your payslip can be confusing, especially with the new SHIF and Housing Levy lines. Here's what every deduction means:

  • Basic Salary / Gross Pay: Your agreed monthly salary before any deductions
  • NSSF: Your retirement savings contribution (6% of gross, max KES 4,320)
  • SHIF: Your health insurance contribution (2.75% of gross, replaced NHIF)
  • AHL (Housing Levy): Affordable Housing Levy (1.5% of gross)
  • PAYE: Income tax calculated on taxable income (gross minus NSSF/SHIF/AHL), less personal relief
  • Net Pay: What actually reaches your bank account

Some payslips also show allowances (house, transport, medical) which may be taxable or non-taxable. Non-cash benefits like company cars have a notional value added to taxable income.
