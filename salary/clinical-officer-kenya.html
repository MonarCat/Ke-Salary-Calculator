Clinical Officer Job Market in Kenya

  • Demand: Very High. Clinical officers are the backbone of Kenya's primary healthcare system. The government and NGO health sector continuously recruit qualified clinical officers.
  • Top Employers: Ministry of Health (MoH), county health departments, Mission for Essential Drugs and Supplies (MEDS), Amref Health Africa, MSF, private clinics, faith-based hospitals (AIC, PCEA, CFK).
  • Top Cities: Nationwide. High demand in rural counties and peri-urban areas.
  • Key skills: Clinical diagnosis, patient management, minor surgery, reproductive health, Kenya Clinical Officers Council (KCOC) registration, IMCI, HMIS.
  • Effective deduction rate: At KES 70,000 gross, you lose 26.3% of your salary to statutory deductions.
  • Annual net pay: KES 619,128 per year.

Clinical Officer Salary Kenya — FAQs

What is the average clinical officer salary in Kenya in 2025?

The average gross salary for a clinical officer in Kenya is approximately KES 65,000–100,000 per month in 2025 in the public sector. A mid-level clinical officer earning KES 70,000 gross takes home KES 51,594 per month after statutory deductions.

How much PAYE does a clinical officer pay in Kenya?

On a KES 70,000 gross salary, a clinical officer pays KES 11,231 in PAYE per month. Taxable income is KES 62,825 after NSSF (KES 4,200), SHIF (KES 1,925), and Housing Levy (KES 1,050) deductions.

How does a clinical officer salary compare to a doctor in Kenya?

A clinical officer typically earns KES 45,000–120,000/month, while a medical doctor earns KES 100,000–300,000/month in the public sector (more in private practice). Clinical officers are mid-level practitioners while doctors complete a full medical degree and internship.

Do clinical officers in Kenya earn allowances in addition to basic salary?

Yes. Government clinical officers typically receive non-practising allowance (NPA), leave allowance, medical allowance, and sometimes commuter and house allowances, all of which are part of gross taxable pay.
